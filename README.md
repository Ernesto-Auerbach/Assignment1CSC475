# The Neutral Ground

A playable, single-player 3D tavern puzzle built with Godot 4.7.2. All models, lighting, characters, and interface are generated by the project. No purchased assets or online services are required.

## Play on Windows

A hidden station visitor can now appear after an unusual sequence of ordinary actions. Listen for a clue in Station Chatter. Its discovery is saved per campaign. The exact solution is in [EASTER_EGG_SPOILERS.md](EASTER_EGG_SPOILERS.md).

[PROMPT_RESPONSE_LOG.md](PROMPT_RESPONSE_LOG.md) records the development prompts and final replies. Project instructions require future work to append to this record.

Extract **TheGame.zip**, open **NeutralGround**, and double-click **PLAY.cmd**. The included official Godot engine runs the game directly; no installation or online service is needed. Keep the **game/** and **engine/** folders beside PLAY.cmd. See [RUN_GAME.md](RUN_GAME.md) for launch instructions alone.

The ZIP's top-level directory is **NeutralGround/**. Some extraction tools automatically create an additional folder named after the archive; choose your intended parent folder as the extraction destination if you want NeutralGround directly inside it. The ZIP cannot override that extractor setting.

To edit, import **NeutralGround/game/project.godot** in Godot 4.7.2 and press F6 with main.tscn open, or F5 to run the project. Other Godot 4 versions may work but were not tested.

## Your shift

Shift one gives you **four simulation minutes to earn 300 credits**, with station trust above zero. Twelve core guests arrive over the first 130 seconds, and six tables seat two guests each. There are five core trade deals. The four new core guests are **Lyra Moss / Bastion Reed** (seed cultures) and **Dax Weller / Elio March** (drone parts). Meeting the displayed goal enables **Close shift**; otherwise, keep serving rotating regulars until time runs out or trust reaches zero.

## Menu and saving

Select a tavern and choose **Delete save** to review its name, shift, credits, time remaining, trust, deals, brawls, and save date. Nothing is removed until you choose **Permanently delete this save**. Cancel or Escape keeps it. Confirming removes that campaign's save and recovery files; other campaigns stay intact. Damaged and backup-only saves can also be removed. If the files change while confirmation is open, the menu asks you to review them again.

Every launch opens the **main menu**. Select a saved tavern to see its shift, credits and goal, remaining time, trust, trade count, incidents, last save time, and recent shift history. **Continue this tavern** restores it; a finished shift opens its results. **Start a new save** lets you name a separate campaign beginning at shift one. There is no fixed slot limit, and creating a campaign never overwrites another one.

Your existing **savegame.dat** appears as **Original tavern**. All campaigns live in **NeutralGround/saves/**, beside the **game/** and **engine/** folders. The existing legacy save and backup were moved into that folder without changing their contents. Keep this folder when replacing the game with a newer download; the release ZIP contains an empty saves folder and never includes personal saves. Browsing the main menu does not modify save files.

**Escape** closes an open conversation first. Otherwise it opens a paused menu with Resume, **Save now [F5]**, **Shift journal [J]**, Restart, **Save & return to main menu**, and Save & quit. Returning to the menu checkpoints the active campaign before clearing the room. If saving fails, your session stays open. Quitting from the main menu does not write an empty session over a save. Restart resets only the current shift, preserving its configuration and campaign journal.

The current campaign automatically saves at shift start, shift end, return to main menu, and normal quit/window close. Press **F5** for a checkpoint without leaving play. Active saves restore positions, orders, scores, timers, guards, pending NPC replies, random state, contracts, audio preference, camera, and progression. Forced process termination cannot trigger the quit save.

Each save has its own **.bak** backup. The main menu identifies backup recovery when the primary is damaged or missing. Unreadable saves are shown without deleting them. New checkpoints are verified before replacing old files, and a damaged primary cannot replace a valid backup.

## Shift journal

Press **J**, use the Escape menu, or open it from shift results to review the last **20 completed attempts** in this campaign. Each entry records credits versus goal, deals, trust, incidents, and completed contracts. The best completed-shift score is kept even after older entries leave the list. Loading a completed shift does not create a duplicate entry. Saves from previous versions begin with an empty journal; their gameplay progress is retained.

## Shift progression

- Shift 1: 4:00, goal 300.
- Shift 2: 6:00, same arrival order as shift 1. Goal = 1,200 when the first score is at most 1,200; otherwise goal = 900, following the requested 4× / 3× rule.
- Every later shift: duration ×1.5, previous goal ×2, a different arrival order. Pair groups shuffle, with partners kept close enough in the schedule to make their trades possible.
- From shift 3, drink, trade, and tip income grows by 4/3 each shift, rounded to half credits. This keeps earning potential growing with doubled goals and 50% longer shifts. Penalties stay fixed.
- A failed shift offers a retry instead of advancing. Completed-shift results are autosaved before advancing.

The seeded balance run earned **1,370 / 300**, **1,710 / 900**, and **3,186.5 / 1,800** in the first three shifts without contracts, completing all five deals with 100 trust each time. Base rewards did not need an increase beyond doubled second rounds.

1. Click a traveler or their manifest entry. Greet them to get their drink order.
2. Ask about cargo and warrants to discover trade opportunities and enemies.
3. Use a table button or click a table to direct the selected traveler. Each table seats two. Guests can be moved again.
4. Once they arrive, serve their requested drink for 15 credits. Incorrect drinks cost 5 credits.
5. Two compatible, served travelers at one table negotiate for eight seconds, then pay a 90-credit commission. Each pair pays once per shift.

Press **N** or **Next guest needing attention** to select the most urgent unserved guest who is ready for an action. Press again to check another waiting guest. Once seated, **F** or the requested-drink button serves their known order. The manual drink choices remain available. The selected guest has a teal floor ring, a live service status, and remembered trade/rival clues from people you have interrogated. The manifest distinguishes arrivals, walking guests, first orders, second rounds, business, and departures.

Selecting a different guest brings their status and service controls back into view and scrolls the manifest to their row. Updates to the same guest keep your scroll position.

After a trade, both partners linger for **12 seconds**, then each tips **20 base credits**, releases their seat, and walks to the airlock. Every guest has one **45% chance per visit** to request a second round. Traders get their chance three seconds after their deal unless they already decided earlier; other guests decide after 28–36 seconds. A request pauses departure and starts a fresh 70-second patience limit. The second round pays **30 base credits—double the first drink**—and starts a new 12-second linger. Declining the chance produces no order or request dialogue.

A trader finishing a second drink waits if their business partner is still available or due to arrive. Completing the deal gives both partners the full 12-second linger. Hunters also finish an outstanding second round and its linger before leaving. A goal-reached cue highlights early closing; a last-call notice marks the final 30 seconds. Results show drink sales, commissions, tips, and deductions separately. Older saves retain their prior earnings as a carried balance in that breakdown.

**Rook and Kestrel must sit together through their introduction and delayed reply.** Both stay until that meeting has happened and all ten other core guests have physically left. Ignoring their drinks still costs trust once, but cannot make them leave prematurely. This departure rule excludes rotating regulars.

Once all twelve core guests have arrived and departures have begun, eight neutral regulars rotate in at 12-second intervals while capacity permits: **Pax, Mira, Tavi, Jun, Ada Prism, Remy Flux, Sol Marr, and Bex Orbit**. Each may return after leaving, with a fresh order and second-round chance. They have distinct roles but no bounty or trade match. Serve them for drink revenue and satisfied-departure tips.

## Station contracts

Press **C** or click **Station contracts** beside the speed controls to review optional jobs. The board pauses play and returns to your previous pause state when closed. Take one job at a time; only actions performed after acceptance count:

| Job | Requirement | Base bonus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Station Broker | Complete three trade deals | 75 credits |
| Keep Them Coming | Serve three requested second rounds | 60 credits |
| A Welcoming Port | Four consecutive satisfied departures | 50 credits |

An unhappy guest or an incident, including a successful security intervention, resets the hospitality streak. You keep the job and can try again. Release an unfinished job to choose another without losing credits; its progress resets. Each job pays automatically once per shift, and completing one frees you to accept another. The brokerage job is unavailable if too few viable trade pairs remain.

Bonuses follow the same income scaling as drinks in later shifts. The contract button shows live progress, and the results ledger lists bonuses separately. Active jobs, progress, and paid jobs are saved. Existing saves start with a fresh optional board. The shift's normal goal and timer are unchanged.

A paced playthrough completed all three jobs and five deals for **1,465 credits**, including **185 contract credits**, with 100 trust.

## Security and shift speed

**Briggs and Ward** stand by the airlock. Press **G**, click **Intervene**, or click an idle guard to dispatch security to an escalating confrontation. The command prioritizes danger involving your selected guest, then the most urgent remaining confrontation. Guards physically travel to the scene. Arriving before the five-second fight threshold resolves the incident for exactly half the normal penalty: **22.5 credits and 12.5 trust**. Late dispatches get no discount. Each incident is charged once.

The same two guards escort blocked departures. A guest waits safely if both guards are busy, then walks out with an assigned guard. Guards return to the door after their task; the control shows how many are available.

Use **1×, 1.25×, 1.5×, or 2×** to change simulation speed. The shift clock, walking, guards, arrivals, patience, negotiation, linger, second-round checks, and tension timers all scale together. Pause and conversations still freeze the simulation. Each new shift defaults to 1×.

Guests and guards retain their movement through path corners. Small simulation steps keep movement and timers together during slow frames; a stall longer than one second is capped for every simulation system, so the timer cannot run away while guests are stuck. Departed guests also stop intercepting clicks at the airlock.

## Operations Board

Press **B** or choose **Operations board** in the Escape menu to plan the room. The board pauses the shift and shows the next three booked arrivals with their estimated arrival times, each table's occupants, active trade progress, and a countdown where a fight is approaching. Arrival estimates use simulation seconds, so the selected shift speed still applies. Once all twelve booked guests have arrived, the forecast explains that regulars rotate in as seats become available.

Up to three buttons identify the unserved guests with the least patience. Choose one to return to that guest's service controls. Closing the board restores your prior pause state.

The board also offers **Pause when a new rivalry turns dangerous**. This optional safety pause is **off by default** and saved separately for each campaign. When enabled, the first dangerous encounter for each rival pair in a shift pauses time and selects the threatened encounter so you can reroute a guest or dispatch security. Press Space to resume. The same pair does not repeatedly pause that shift, including after saving and loading; a new shift permits a fresh warning.

## Routes and safe departures

The arrival line advances smoothly whenever a waiting guest is assigned a table or leaves. Everyone behind closes the gap in arrival order, even when someone from the middle is seated. Returning regulars join the back; an empty line starts again at the front marker. Queue movement follows the selected shift speed and pauses with gameplay. Its order is saved, and older saves recover their line order from guest positions.

Select **Safe**, **Central**, or **South**, then hover a table in the room or its sidebar button. The floor line previews the exact route that will be assigned; **red sections pass within 4.4 meters of a rival**. Central uses the middle aisle; South uses the lower aisle. Safe searches for a furniture-aware detour around current rival positions. If no safe route exists, its fallback is still shown in red—choose another route, wait, or move the rival. Seating routes are fixed when assigned, so moving rivals can change their safety afterward. Space pauses planning.

Exits automatically replan every 0.6 simulation seconds using current rival and guest positions and check each movement step against rival clearance. They aim for 4.8 meters of clearance. If every safe route is blocked, a guard is assigned and physically accompanies the guest. Departing guests never generate brawl penalties, including when a rival moves into their route. Escort protection only applies to departures; your seating choices still carry normal risk.

## Read the room

- Billboarded drink icons distinguish whiskey, lager, coffee, and nebula fizz after an order is taken, including second rounds.
- Circular patience meters drain smoothly from teal to orange, then flash red for the final quarter. They disappear once served.
- Blue floor rings indicate normal tables. Green pulses and a filling outer arc track the eight-second negotiation. Amber/red flashes show proximity tension and take priority over trading.
- A heart by a guest's name indicates a satisfied linger before departure.

Bounty hunters recognize their targets within 4.4 meters, including while walking. A five-second warning gives you time to separate them. A brawl costs 45 credits and 25 trust; security then prevents that pair fighting again. Waiting guests remain protected by airlock security until assigned a table.

Guests leave after 70 seconds without a drink, costing 10 trust. Serving a guest ends their patience countdown. Conversations pause the simulation; selecting someone alone does not. Commands issued while manually paused execute when you resume.

## Conversations and sound

Specific pairs have custom exchanges, including Rook/Kestrel, Iona/Nix, Vex/Ash, Sable/Oren, Pax/Mira, Tavi/Jun, and Iona/Tavi. Other conversations draw on each participant's role. Replies wait **three simulation seconds** after the first line; a conversation cooldown prevents competing exchanges. Moving or departing cancels an outdated reply. Full dialogue appears in Station Chatter as well as overhead bubbles.

Repeated ambient exchanges stay in Station Chatter; character introductions and important events still use overhead bubbles so orders remain easier to see.

Seating a guest with someone hunting their friend produces overheard intel. For example, Iona notices Kestrel's energy holster and warns that Ash may be in danger. Clues happen before the target needs to enter the encounter, and fire once per pairing per visit.

Procedural audio adds a 587→880 Hz cash chime for successful trades, a 110 Hz square-wave wrong-order buzzer, and an 80–100 Hz tension thrum whose pulses accelerate as the fight approaches. No external audio assets are used.

## Controls

- Left click and release: select guest / assign selected guest to a table
- Hold left click and drag the room: pan the map at any zoom level; UI buttons and scroll areas keep their usual behavior
- Home: recenter the map without changing zoom or rotation
- Space: pause or resume
- Hold Q / E: smoothly rotate the camera; release to stop (independent of shift speed)
- Mouse wheel: zoom
- Escape: close conversation first; otherwise pause menu / resume
- G / Intervene / click idle guard: dispatch security
- F / requested-drink button: serve the selected guest's known order once seated
- N / Next guest needing attention: select an urgent unserved guest
- C / Station contracts: choose or review an optional job; Escape returns to play
- M: mute or restore sound effects; also available in the pause menu and retained in saves
- F5: save the current campaign without leaving gameplay
- J: review this campaign's shift journal
- B: open the Operations Board; review arrivals, tables, urgent orders, and optional safety pauses
- Speed buttons: 1×, 1.25×, 1.5×, 2×
- ?: house rules

## Prototype scope

Includes escalating shifts, persistent saves, early completion, replay, scripted conversations, table turnover, second rounds, route selection and previews, proximity recognition, trading, guards, brawl animation, and synthesized feedback sounds. The player acts as the keeper through an overhead interface. There is no walking player avatar or freeform AI dialogue. Navigation uses a furniture-aware floor graph, selectable aisles, and live replanning for departures.
